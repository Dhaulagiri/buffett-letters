# Owner letter style

Help a business explain a completed period to its owners in original, clear prose. This package studies communication techniques in historical shareholder letters; it does not write as Warren Buffett or imply Berkshire Hathaway endorsement. Read [references/sources.md](references/sources.md) when attributing a technique to the research.

## Start with the record

Establish the audience, business, period, purpose, and whether the company is real or fictional. For a real company, ask for or locate the financial statements and material facts needed for the requested claims. Maintain a source ledger pairing every consequential figure and event with its source and period. Mark missing information as an open question; do not fill it with plausible inventions. A fictional example may use invented facts when it is clearly labeled fictional and its calculations agree.

## Choose the work

- For an initial draft or substantial rewrite, use `owner-letter-architecture`; add `owner-letter-voice` for clarity and tone.
- For results and metrics, use `owner-letter-performance`.
- For cash use, financing, acquisitions, dividends, or repurchases, use `owner-letter-capital-allocation`.
- For setbacks, uncertainty, and changed judgments, use `owner-letter-risk-and-errors`.
- Before delivery, use `owner-letter-review`.

Use only the specialists relevant to the request. Preserve a user's chosen format and scope.

## Delivery contract

Provide the draft or review, the period and audience it addresses, material source or assumption notes, unresolved factual questions, and review status. Distinguish reported facts, management interpretations, and forward-looking judgments in the writing itself. If the record cannot support a claim, narrow or omit it and tell the user what is missing.
