# Owner letter review

Read the draft against its source ledger or explicit fictional assumptions. Report actionable findings with the claim, evidence or calculation, significance, and a concrete fix. Do not treat missing source access as proof a claim is false; mark it unverified. Check the following where relevant:

- Figures, units, denominators, arithmetic, rounding, table-to-prose agreement, and like-for-like periods.
- Attribution of events and claims to the correct company, year, author, and source.
- Whether appendices, guest contributions, tables, or extracted columns have been attributed and interpreted correctly.
- Whether reported results, adjusted measures, interpretations, forecasts, and hypotheticals are distinguishable.
- Whether material bad news, costs, failed decisions, liquidity pressures, and uncertainty receive proportionate attention.
- Whether any quote, anecdote, source link, or historical claim was invented or copied without clearance.
- Whether the document impersonates a named writer or suggests endorsement.

Prioritize errors that could change an owner's understanding. If there are no findings, state which inputs were reviewed and which could not be verified. For a fictional example, check internal consistency and clear fictional labeling. After revisions, recheck affected claims and calculations; do not simply mark earlier findings resolved.
