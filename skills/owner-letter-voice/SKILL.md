# Owner letter voice

Write as the actual author or organization, addressing the stated audience. Favor ordinary words, concrete business details, and explanations of specialized terms proportionate to their importance. Use first person only when the author can genuinely own the judgment or action. Do not imitate Warren Buffett's signature phrases, anecdotes, jokes, or persona, and do not claim endorsement.

Prefer an explanation to an adjective: show which operating fact makes a result strong, weak, or surprising. A comparison or analogy should clarify a specific mechanism; remove it when it distracts, overstates similarity, or hides a caveat. Keep humor optional and subordinate to the stakes of the subject.

Separate confidence levels in the prose. State known figures directly, label estimates, and explain what would change a judgment. Replace polished evasions with a precise description of the decision, the evidence for it, and the remaining uncertainty. Preserve the author's true view rather than manufacturing candor or certainty.

Check authorship before treating an appendix, quoted letter, remembrance, or guest contribution as evidence of the principal writer's voice. Length, humor, and anecdote vary substantially and are optional, not a formula to imitate.

Example, fictional: “Orders rose 12%, while delivery cost per order rose 18%. The extra volume has not yet paid for the new route.” This is more informative than “We delivered outstanding momentum despite temporary headwinds.”
