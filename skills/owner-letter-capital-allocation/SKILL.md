# Owner letter capital allocation

For each consequential use of cash or capital, identify the amount and period when available, the relevant alternatives, and the reason for choosing it. Describe the expected benefit and the evidence available at decision time to the extent the authorized record supports them. Explain financing costs, dilution, liquidity needs, and opportunity costs when material to the choice. State when confidentiality, competitive sensitivity, or grouped reporting limits the detail. Treat this as an editorial checklist, not a rigid annual-letter template.

Distinguish a decision's rationale from its later result. An acquisition, reinvestment, debt repayment, dividend, or repurchase can be reasonable under some assumptions and disappointing under others. State the assumptions and what subsequent evidence would test them. Avoid hindsight certainty and do not convert a historical pattern into current investment advice.

Growth alone does not establish that capital created owner value. Include the price paid, financing or dilution, incremental capital required, and expected economics when those facts are material and available.

If comparing opportunities, make the comparison concrete: expected cash commitment, time horizon, risk, and constraints. Do not invent a precise return estimate where the business lacks one. When facts are unavailable, frame the issue as a question for management rather than an asserted conclusion.
