# Owner letter architecture

Start from what the owners need to understand about the period. As a coverage check, ask: What changed? How did the business perform? What decisions mattered? What went poorly? What remains uncertain? Arrange the answers around the year's material facts. Do not force a section where the available record has nothing useful to say. These questions are a design aid, not a historical template.

An effective opening identifies the period, the business's main development, and the result that needs explanation. Give readers a short map of the economic engine before a table of figures if the business model is unfamiliar. Use headings to make longer updates navigable. Place a metric beside the context required to interpret it, including its comparator, unit, period, and material limitations.

Do not force a metric-first opening or a fixed annual template. A succession issue, loss, changed scorecard, or other owner-relevant obligation may deserve the opening instead.

Connect decisions to outcomes without assigning causality that the evidence cannot support. Put material adverse information where a reader will encounter it; do not bury it after an optimistic closing. When an outlook is material and supported, explain its assumptions, constraints, and open questions where it best helps the owner; do not turn it into a promise of performance.

For a fictional worked example, label the company and figures fictional. For a real business, retain only supported claims and flag missing data for the owner. Apply `owner-letter-review` before delivery.
