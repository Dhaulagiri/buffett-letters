# Owner letter performance

Choose a focused set of measures proportionate to the business and the period. For each measure, supply its definition, unit, time span, denominator where applicable, comparator, and source. Recompute percentages from underlying values when available. Explain changes in scope or accounting that make periods less comparable. The exact metrics must fit the current business.

Keep reported numbers distinct from a management interpretation of underlying economics. If using an adjusted measure, show what was excluded and why; retain the reported result nearby. Do not treat cash flow, earnings, revenue, and owner value as interchangeable. Explain a business-specific driver instead of asserting that a favorable number is intrinsically good.

Do not preserve a familiar metric after it stops representing the business well. Explain why a scorecard changed or why a usually reported measure was omitted, and provide continuity where a fair comparison remains possible.

A compact table may help when it lets a reader compare periods at a glance and the format preserves column relationships. Put units and period labels in the header, and state any rounding rule. Explain how to interpret dense or adjusted tables in prose. Reconcile a headline claim to the table. If the figures are fictional, label them as such and check their arithmetic. If a required source is missing, use an explicit placeholder or omit the claim.

Example, fictional: Revenue of $11.2 million versus $10.0 million is 12% growth. A claim of 20% growth would fail review.
